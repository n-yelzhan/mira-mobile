## Key messages

1. **Strong, steady growth.** Real GDP grew 7.0% a year on average in 2021–2025 and is projected at about 6.8% a year in 2026–2030, led by energy, construction and ICT.
2. **A resource and farm base, diversifying.** Agriculture, mining, energy and manufacturing make 55% of GDP. ICT is only 5% of GDP but grew 18% in 2025. High-tech manufacturing and tech exports are small but growing about 25% a year.
3. **A gateway between the steppe and the sea.** Two landlocked neighbors depend on Mediland's railways, roads and port. Transit, a new air hub and tourism (3.1 million visitors in 2025) are becoming growth engines.
4. **A young, diverse population.** 22.0 million people of five main peoples, median age 27, fertility rising to 3.0. About 330,000 young people enter working age every year, so job creation is the main policy test.
5. **A large country with wide gaps.** The far ends of the country are a full day's drive apart, and a fifth of the land has no settlements. Household income in the richest region is almost three times that of the poorest.
6. **The green agenda drives the digital one.** Water stress, air pollution and climate risks push the country toward clean power, electric transport and environmental monitoring, which raise demand for electricity, IT and connectivity.

## History

The lands of Mediland have long been a crossroads between the steppe and the sea. Medilandi farmers settled the Mira valley, Tavari shepherds lived in the northern and western mountains, Oruki horse herders roamed the eastern steppe, and Selenese fishermen worked the coast. Caravan routes from the east ended at Port Selen, founded as a trading harbor about 600 years ago; the peoples traded, intermarried and shared markets and festivals. In the 19th century the region came under the rule of the Kordanian Empire to the north. Kordana built the first railways, opened the Dorhan mines and brought engineers and workers to the new industrial towns, the roots of today's Kordanian community.

Mediland became independent on September 15, 1961, and a year later moved its capital to Veridan, a new planned city on the Mira. The first decades brought state-led industrialization: the Karvost and Ferrum Belt plants, the Mira dams and irrigation canals. The fall in commodity prices in the late 1990s hit hard: output shrank, many people left to work abroad and births dropped sharply in the 2000s. A new constitution (1995) and market reforms from 2005 opened the economy to trade and investment. Since then Mediland has grown steadily, opened the Nova Lume tech city (2019) and the Mira Gateway airport, and now sees itself as a bridge between its neighbors and a country moving from industry to technology.

## Climate

Mediland has four clear seasons and big differences between regions. Extreme weather comes every few years and is becoming more frequent.

| Season | Months | Steppe and plains | Mountains | Coast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Winter | December–February | −15 to −5 °C, snow, steppe blizzards | Below −20 °C on the heights, passes close | +5 to +12 °C, rain |
| Spring | March–May | Short, fast warming | Snowmelt: floods and mudflows | Mild, blossom season |
| Summer | June–August | Hot and dry, +30 to +40 °C | Cool, +15 to +25 °C | Warm and humid, +28 to +32 °C |
| Autumn | September–November | Dry, sunny, harvest | Early snow from October | Warm until October, sea storms |

- Precipitation: mountains 800–1,200 mm a year; plains 350–500 mm; steppe 200–300 mm; coast about 600 mm.
- Recent extreme events: drought in 2018 (grain harvest down 30%); an earthquake of magnitude 6.1 near Kesh in 2020 (the north is a seismic zone); spring floods on the Mira in 2023; a heat wave with power cuts in 2024; hard winters with livestock losses in the eastern steppe about once in five years.
- Climate change: hotter summers, shrinking glaciers and bigger swings from year to year.

## Administrative divisions and time zones

Mediland has 20 regions: 19 provinces and the Veridan Capital District, which has special status. Each region has a governor appointed by the president and an elected regional council; regions are split into about 210 districts. Region codes R01–R20 are the keys used in all data tables of the Mediland universe.

There are two time zones. Sixteen regions, including Veridan and Port Selen, live on **UTC+5**. The four eastern steppe regions (R12–R15) live on **UTC+6**, in step with trade partner Zaravan. The country is not very wide, so the second zone is a political choice; trains on the East–West Corridor cross the zone line near Sarnhold.

## Population and society

The population grew from 20.3 million in 2021 to 22.0 million in 2026, about 1.6% a year, and a second baby boom is under way.

- **Fertility.** It fell to 2.2 children per woman in the mid-2000s after the economic crisis, rose to 2.8 in 2021 and to 3.0 in 2026. About 530,000 babies are born each year.
- **Households.** 5.3 million households, 4.2 people on average; three generations often live together in rural areas.
- **Urbanization.** The urban share rose from 52% in 2010 to 60%, as young people move to Port Selen, Veridan and Nova Lume.

> **Age structure, 2026 (share of population):** 0–4: 12%; 5–14: 20%; 15–24: 15% (born in the low-birth 2000s); 25–34: 17%; 35–44: 14%; 45–54: 10%; 55–64: 7%; 65+: 5%. 32% are under 15; median age 27.

- **Labor force.** 63% of people are of working age (15–64); the labor force is about 9.2 million.
- **Jobs by sector.** Agriculture 24%, industry 24%, services 52%. Farming employs far more people than its share of GDP, so farm productivity is a key to higher incomes.
- **Youth.** About 330,000 young people reach working age each year; youth unemployment is 14%.

**Peoples and languages.** Mediland's peoples have lived side by side for centuries. Mixed marriages are common, and the constitution protects regional languages.

**Middle class, travel and expats.**

- **Middle class:** about 1.2 million households (24%, about 5.5 million people), mostly in Port Selen, Veridan, Nova Lume and Karvost. They own cars, buy on credit, pay cashless and holiday abroad at least once a year.
- **Business travel:** about 480,000 foreign business visitors came in 2025, up 15% a year. Medilanders make about 300,000 business trips abroad a year, mostly to Kordana, Corvane and Zaravan.
- **Expats:** about 65,000 foreign specialists live in Mediland with their families, working in mining (Dorhan), energy (Kesh, Oruk), IT (Nova Lume) and finance (Port Selen). Nova Lume and Port Selen have international schools.
- **Diaspora:** about 600,000 Medilanders work abroad, mostly in Kordana and Corvane; remittances are about 5% of GDP.

## Recent economic developments

Growth reached 7.2% in 2025, the fifth year close to 7%, driven by investment in energy, mines, transport and housing and by fast-growing digital services.

> **Real GDP growth, % per year:** 2021 6.2; 2022 7.4; 2023 6.9; 2024 7.3; 2025 7.2 (2021–2025 average 7.0); forecast 2026 7.1; 2027 7.0; 2028 6.8; 2029 6.7; 2030 6.5.

- **Demand side.** Investment is about 31% of GDP, mostly in power plants, mines, railways, roads and housing. Household spending grows with wages and remittances; exports grew about 9% in 2025 on higher volumes of copper, gas and grain.
- **Supply side.** A record grain harvest, two new wind farms in Oruk and a new copper mine in Dorhan added to output. Trade and transport kept pace with the economy but lost share to faster sectors.
- **Structure.** The economy still rests on farms, mines, power and factories, while ICT, construction and finance grow fastest.

> **GDP by sector, 2025 (share of GDP; real growth 2025):** Agriculture, forestry, fishing 20% (+4.1); Trade and transport 17% (+6.0); Manufacturing 16% (+7.0); Mining, oil and gas 13% (+8.5); Public services, education, health 8% (+4.0); Energy and utilities 6% (+9.2); Construction 6% (+10.5); Finance and insurance 6% (+9.0); ICT and digital services 5% (+18.4); Other services 3% (+5.5).

- **Port Selen.** A 600-year-old harbor city on steep hills. Narrow streets and a crowded old town sit beside a strip of glass towers on the waterfront, home to the central bank, the stock exchange and bank head offices. Traffic and housing costs are the highest in the country; an automated light rail line is under construction.
- **Veridan.** Built as the capital in the 1960s on the open river plain. Wide avenues, green belts, a large government quarter and campus-style universities: low density and lots of space, but long car commutes. Commuter trains link it to Nova Lume and the airport.
- **Karvost.** A city laid out around plants and rail yards: machinery, car and electric bus assembly, worker districts with mid-rise blocks and a busy rail junction. Old plants are being refitted with robots.
- **Nova Lume.** A tech city opened in 2019 between the river and the hills. High-rise offices and flats, a tech park with tax breaks, data centers and electronics plants, and the country's first driverless light rail line. It has the youngest population in the country and grows up, not out.
- **Teral.** The crossroads of the north–south railway, the main highways and the Mira waterway: a dry port, free trade zone, warehouses and ring roads spread over a wide area.
- **Sarnhold.** The grain capital, with the national grain exchange, elevators, farm machinery dealers and an agricultural university that tests drones and sensors.
- **Dorhan.** A mining town in a narrow mountain valley, with mine offices, ore plants and rotation camps where workers live two weeks at a time.
- **Coral Bay.** A 40 km strip of beaches, hotels and fishing villages; its population nearly doubles in June–September.
- **Kesh.** A small town below the Kesh dam, the largest of five hydropower plants on the upper Mira. It hosts the national grid control center and a data-center park that uses cheap power and cool mountain air.
- **Mira Gateway.** A new town around the international airport, between Veridan, Port Selen and the coast: terminals, hotels, an air cargo center and conference halls.

Other notable towns include Saltmere (fishing port), Eastgate (border hub with Zaravan), Qasra (gas and border hub with Belmora) and Oruk (wind-farm service town).

## Transport, logistics and transit

Mediland sits where an east–west land route from the steppe meets the sea. Six hubs and four main rail lines connect its regions and neighbors, and the network is being electrified.

| Hub | Region | Type | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Port Selen | R02 | Seaport | Main container and bulk port: 1.3 million TEU in 2025, 2.5 million by 2030; about 18% of cargo is Zaravan transit; ferries to Corvane |
| Teral | R17 | Inland dry port | Junction of the north–south railway, main highways and the Mira waterway; free trade zone; barges to Port Selen |
| Mira Gateway International Airport | R16 | Air hub | The only international hub: 7.8 million passengers in 2025, 2.9 million of them in transit; target 15 million by 2030; air cargo; main gateway for tourists |
| Eastgate | R12 | Border hub with Zaravan | Rail and road terminal, truck parks, grain and container trains |

**Main arteries.**

- **Railways** (3,700 km, 38% electrified, 60% by 2030):
  - East–West Corridor: Eastgate – Sarnhold – Karvost – Veridan – Mira Gateway – Port Selen, about 770 km, double-tracked and electrified by 2029.
  - North–South line: Kesh – Teral – Veridan – Mira Gateway – Coral Bay, to be extended to Kordana through the Kesh tunnel.
  - Southeast line: Veridan – Ferrum Belt – Qasra – Belmora border.
  - Coastal line: Westreach – Saltmere – Port Selen – Coral Bay; ore line Dorhan – Teral (freight only).
- **Highways:** M1 East–West (Eastgate – Veridan – Port Selen); M2 North–South (Tavar Pass – Kesh – Teral – Veridan – Coral Bay); M3 Southeast (Veridan – Qasra – Belmora); M4 Coastal (Westreach – Port Selen – Coral Bay); M5 West (Teral – Dorhan – Halvaria, partly closed in winter).
- **Waterways:** the Mira is navigable for 340 km from Teral to the sea (grain, cement and container barges); small boats on the lower Tavar.
- **Air:** Mira Gateway is the international hub; domestic airports in Veridan (Veridan City), Dorhan and Sarnhold; a seasonal airport at Coral Bay; small airfields in Kesh, Tavarin, Kanbar, Qasra and Oruk serve remote regions, mining and energy sites.
- **Pipelines:** gas from the Southern Dry Steppe to Belmora and Kordana.

**Cleaner, electric transport.**

- **Commuter trains are being modernized:** 120 new electric trains by 2028 around Veridan, Nova Lume and Mira Gateway and on the Port Selen – Coral Bay coast; 45% of the suburban fleet is already renewed.
- **Light rail:** a 12 km driverless light rail line with 10 stations has run in Nova Lume since 2024. Lines in Port Selen (22 km, 2028) and Veridan, linked to Nova Lume (2029), are under construction.
- **Electric buses and cars:** electric buses are 18% of city bus fleets (target 50% by 2030); trolleybuses run in Veridan and Karvost; intercity electric buses run on M1 and M2. Electric cars were 4% of new car sales in 2025, up from 0.5% in 2021, with fast chargers along M1 and M2.

**Distances and travel times (2026).** The far ends of the country are about 1,000 km apart, a full day's drive.

**Transit potential.** Mediland is the shortest route to the sea for Zaravan and Belmora and part of an overland route between East Asia and the Selen Sea. The Kesh tunnel (2030) will open a route from Kordana to the sea. Transit earns about USD 0.9 billion a year, and the government aims to double it by 2030.

## Tourism

Tourism is growing fast from a low base: international arrivals rose from 1.2 million in 2021 to 3.1 million in 2025 and are forecast at 5.5 million by 2030. Receipts are about USD 2.6 billion (2.7% of GDP).

- **Who comes:** visitors from Kordana, Zaravan and Corvane, plus transit passengers on stopovers at Mira Gateway.
- **What they come for:** Coral Bay beaches; trekking and skiing in High Tavar and Kesh Valleys; Tessa Lake; the old harbor of Port Selen; steppe tours and the Horse Games in Oruk.
- **Domestic tourism:** about 9.5 million trips a year, to the coast in summer and the mountains in winter.
- **Outbound travel:** trips abroad by Medilanders rose from 1.3 million in 2021 to 2.4 million in 2025, driven by the middle class.
- **Bottlenecks:** few mid-range hotels outside Coral Bay and Port Selen, slow mountain roads, a shortage of seasonal staff.

## Environment

Water stress, industrial air pollution and climate shocks are now economic risks. The government's answer, the Green Growth Strategy 2035, relies on clean power, electric transport and measuring the environment in real time.

| Pressure | Where | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water stress | Mira basin (R10, R16, R17, R18); Tavar and Oruk shared with neighbors | The Mira feeds about 70% of irrigated land; glaciers are shrinking; cotton and rice use water inefficiently |
| Air pollution | Karvost (R03), Ferrum Belt (R19), central Port Selen | Coal-fired heat, steel and cement plants, traffic; winter smog often exceeds national limits 3–4 times |
| Land degradation | Southern Dry Steppe (R15), Great East Steppe (R12) | Overgrazing and drought spread dry land and dust storms |
| Mining impact | Dorhan Highlands (R04), Dor river | Tailings dams and river water quality downstream |
| Natural hazards | Mountains (R04, R09, R10, R11), coast (R06–R08) | Earthquakes, spring mudflows and floods, heat waves, storm surges |

**Green Growth Strategy 2035, main targets**

- Renewables from 24% of electricity in 2025 to 45% by 2035.
- Coal heat in Karvost replaced by gas and heat pumps by 2032; emission reporting for all large plants.
- Half of city buses electric by 2030; 60% of railways electrified.
- Water-saving irrigation on half of irrigated land by 2035.
- A national monitoring network: air stations in every city, river gauges, glacier and mudflow sensors, seismic stations, wildfire cameras in the steppe.

The green agenda is also a driver of IT and connectivity. Smart irrigation, grid balancing for wind and solar, and early-warning systems all depend on sensors and data links, often in remote mountain and steppe areas with weak coverage today.

## Energy

Electricity demand grows about 5.8% a year, faster than GDP, and the power system is moving from coal to gas and renewables.

- Generation was 58 TWh in 2025: gas 38%, coal 36%, hydro 17%, wind 5%, solar 2%, other 2%.
- Demand peaks in July–August (air conditioning) and January (heating). In the 2024 heat wave, parts of the plains had rolling power cuts.
- New demand comes from data centers, electrified railways and buses, high-tech plants and air conditioning.
- Tariffs are below cost and grid losses are 11%, so investment in the grid lags behind generation.
- New capacity: wind in Oruk, solar in the Qasra Sands, more hydro on the Mira and a gas plant near Karvost.

## Toward a post-industrial economy

The national program Digital Mediland 2030 aims to lift ICT from 5% to 9% of GDP and services from 39% to 45% of GDP by 2030. The goal is not to replace farms, mines and factories but to make them more productive with data, automation and high-tech production.

| Area | What IT is needed for | Main places |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agriculture | Precision farming, smart irrigation, machinery telematics, livestock tracking | Sarn Plains, Mira valley, steppe regions |
| Mining and gas | Remote and automated operations, equipment monitoring, safety systems | Dorhan, Southern Dry Steppe |
| Energy | Smart grid, forecasting and balancing wind and solar, smart meters | Kesh, Oruk, Southern Dry Steppe |
| Manufacturing | Automation, quality control, predictive maintenance, electronics production | Karvost, Ferrum Belt, Nova Lume |
| Transport and logistics | Digital customs, cargo tracking, train control, airport systems, e-commerce | Teral, Port Selen, Mira Gateway, Eastgate |
| Finance | Mobile banking, e-wallets, cashless payments, credit scoring | Port Selen, nationwide |
| Public services | E-government, digital ID, online registers | Veridan, nationwide |
| Health and education | Telemedicine and online schooling for remote villages | Mountain and steppe regions |
| Environment | Monitoring networks and early-warning systems | Nationwide |

**Education and skills.** Education levels are rising fast but unevenly: big cities are close to upper-middle-income standards, remote villages lag behind.

- Literacy is 99%; secondary school completion 91% (cities 97%, villages 83%); 25% of adults have higher education.
- 62 universities, the strongest in Veridan, Nova Lume and Port Selen; about 9,000 IT graduates a year, short of demand.
- Teachers are short in mountain and steppe schools; an online school program has run since 2022.
- About a quarter of adults speak English well, mostly young and urban.

**Attitudes to new technology** vary sharply across the country.

| Group | Share of adults | Who | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Early adopters | about 20% | Young urban professionals and students in Nova Lume, Port Selen, Veridan | First to try new apps, devices and payments; travel; switch providers easily |
| Pragmatic majority | about 55% | Families in towns and the central plains | Adopt when it is cheaper or simpler, often after friends and relatives do |
| Conservatives | about 25% | Older people, rural mountain and steppe communities | Prefer cash, face-to-face service and their own language; loyal but slow to change |

Connectivity underpins every area above, including in thinly populated regions; the connectivity market and technologies are covered in a separate review.

## Services, finance and business climate

Services, payments and the business climate are improving fast in the big cities, while remote regions still rely on cash, distant hospitals and informal work.

- **Health.** Health spending is 3.8% of GDP. Modern hospitals are concentrated in Veridan, Port Selen and Nova Lume; in the mountains the nearest hospital can be 3–5 hours away. A telemedicine pilot links 400 village clinics to city hospitals.
- **Finance and payments.** 68% of adults have a bank account (cities 82%, villages 49%) and 55% have a payment card. Cash was 58% of retail payments in 2025, down from 80% in 2020; e-wallets have 6.1 million users. Consumer credit grows about 20% a year; the policy rate is 9%.
- **Informal economy.** About 25% of jobs and 18% of GDP are informal, mostly in farming, small trade and construction; this work is cash-based and seasonal.
- **Housing.** About 9 million m² of housing is built each year, with new districts in Port Selen, Veridan and Nova Lume; a state mortgage program lends at 7%.
- **Business climate.** Special economic zones: Nova Lume Tech Park (0% profit tax for 10 years), Teral Free Trade Zone, Mira Gateway Airport City and Qasra Energy Zone. Corporate tax is 15%, VAT 12%. Foreign businesspeople get visa-free entry for up to 30 days.
- **Governance.** About 70% of public services are online; digital ID launched in 2023 and has 8 million users. A regional development program (2025–2030) funds roads, water, schools and clinics in mountain and steppe regions; an anti-corruption agency has worked since 2021.

**Downside risks**

- Commodity prices: copper, gold and gas are about 45% of exports.
- Water and climate: a dry year on the Mira would cut farm output and hydropower at the same time; disputes over the shared Tavar and Oruk rivers.
- Natural disasters: earthquakes in the north, floods, drought.
- Jobs and regions: if job creation slows, youth unemployment and migration from remote regions rise; regional gaps persist.
- Energy: late new capacity would cause power shortages in peak months.
- Dependence on Kordana for technology; shortage and outflow of IT specialists.

**Upside risks**

- Faster transit on the East–West Corridor and the Kesh rail tunnel.
- Growth of the Mira Gateway air hub and of tourism.
- Stronger high-tech and IT exports.
- New mining and renewable projects attracting foreign investment.
