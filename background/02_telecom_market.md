## At a glance

Mediland's telecom market is worth about USD 1.91 bn in 2026 (1.8% of GDP) and grows about 7% a year. It has 23.8 M active mobile SIMs (108 per 100 people), 15.0 M unique mobile users and 2.25 M homes with broadband. Five operators compete; Sunline leads mobile with 35% of SIMs, and Mira Mobile, our company, is second with 30%.

Three things shape the market. Data use per smartphone grows about 25% a year, so capacity in big cities is the main cost. A third of the territory has weak or no signal, and building there rarely pays back. Home internet over mobile routers is the fastest-growing service (+29% a year), because wired lines reach only a quarter of homes.

## Market drivers and constraints

Demand grows on every side, but geography caps how far networks can reach: about 0.7 M people (3%) have no mobile signal at home and 1.8 M (8%) have no 4G.

**What drives demand**

- Population +1.6% a year; about 330,000 people reach working age every year; median age 27.
- Cities grow: urban share 52% (2010) to 60% (2026). New districts in Veridan, Port Selen, Nova Lume and Teral need new sites every year.
- Data use per smartphone: 4.8 GB per month (2023) to 9.6 GB (2026), about 14 GB expected in 2028.
- Digital Mediland 2030: e-government (70% of services online), digital ID (8 M users), mobile banking and e-wallets (6.1 M users), online school, telemedicine.
- Business demand: mining automation (Dorhan, Qasra), smart grid and smart meters, ports and rail corridors, farm drones. M2M SIMs grow about 25% a year.
- Tourism (3.6 M arrivals in 2026) and business visitors bring roaming and short-term SIMs, mostly in summer on the coast.

**What limits supply**

- 19% of the land has no permanent settlements, and another 30% has fewer than 5 people per km2. Often there is no village, power line or signal for 80-150 km.
- Mountains cover 32% of the land but hold 10% of people. Valleys block radio signal; passes close in winter, so sites cannot be serviced.
- Steppe covers 35% of the land with 11% of people: long distances, hard winters about 1 year in 5.
- Power: cuts during heat waves (2024) and peak demand; remote sites need diesel or solar with batteries.
- Natural hazards: the 2020 earthquake near Kesh and the 2023 Mira floods damaged sites and fiber.
- Low incomes in the mountains and western coast (household income USD 520-640 per month in R08, R09, R11, R12), cash in 58% of retail payments, and about 25% of people slow to adopt new technology.

**What the networks must do next**

- Add capacity in dense, old, hilly Port Selen and in high-rise Nova Lume (small cells, 5G).
- Cover new city districts, the East-West rail corridor (electrified by 2029) and the Kesh tunnel (2030).
- Reach remote areas through shared towers, state Universal Service money and satellite links, not standalone builds.

### Sunline (#4, market leader)

The first and still the largest mobile operator: best coverage, best quality, highest prices. 8.36 M SIMs (35.2%), revenue USD 534 M.

- **Company.** First GSM licence in 1998, launched 1999. Head office in Veridan, regional hubs in Port Selen, Karvost and Sarnhold. Owners: a Kordanian telecom group (40%, strategic investor) and a local industrial holding (35%); 25% listed on the Port Selen exchange.
- **Customers.** Urban middle class, premium users, large companies and government; strong in every region, the only real choice in many mountain valleys.
- **Services.** B2C: prepaid and postpaid plans, family groups, unlimited 5G, home routers, fiber in Veridan and Nova Lume, TV app, SunPay e-wallet and micro-loans. B2B: corporate lines, M2M and IoT platform, cloud and data center (Veridan), cybersecurity, private networks.

| Spectrum | 800, 900, 1800, 2100, 2600 MHz; 3.5 GHz (100 MHz) |
| Radio network | 2G/4G/5G; Ericsson in cities, Nokia in regions; 3G switched off in 2025 |
| Core | 4G core plus 5G non-standalone; cloud-native 5G standalone core in pilot (Veridan, Nova Lume) |
| Transport | own fiber rings in 4 cities, microwave in regions, leased TransMed fiber between cities |
| IT | vendor convergent billing and CRM, mobile app with 4.1 M users, SunPay platform |
| Data and AI | data lake on Hadoop moving to a cloud lakehouse; models for churn, next best offer, credit scoring |

**Network plans to 2030**

- 5G in 15 cities by 2028 and 5G standalone across the network by 2028; network slicing for ports and mines.
- About 1,200 new sites, half of them small cells in Port Selen and Nova Lume.
- Direct-to-phone satellite service for mountains and highways from 2028; 2G switch-off in 2030.

**5-year strategy (2027-2031)**

1. Defend the premium base: best network, 5G first, bundles with SunPay and TV.
2. Grow beyond connectivity: e-wallet, micro-loans, cloud and IoT for business.
3. Lead in remote coverage through satellite and shared towers, without building alone.

**Competitive advantages:** widest coverage (4G for 91% of people, signal on 64% of land); strongest brand and highest NPS among big players; the largest shop network; scale in spectrum and sites.

### MedTel (#3, old national operator)

The former state monopoly: owns the fixed network and the national backbone, sells cheap basic services with state support. 3.81 M mobile SIMs (16.0%), 2.64 M fixed voice lines, 0.87 M wired broadband lines; revenue USD 474 M.

- **Company.** Founded in 1962 as the State Post, Telegraph and Telephone; became Mediland Telecom JSC in 1998; 49% listed in 2008, the state keeps 51%. Mobile brand MedTel Mobile since 2004. Head office in Veridan, an office in every one of the 20 regions.
- **Customers.** Households in older buildings, older people, rural areas, government offices; about 0.6 M users on social tariffs; prepaid pay-per-use phones in small regions.
- **Services.** Fixed voice (88% of the market), DSL and fiber internet (60% of wired broadband), basic IPTV, simple mobile plans and pay-per-use, social tariffs. Wholesale: backbone capacity, national roaming for Nova Wave, tower lease, Universal Service towers. Secure network for government.

- 4G on 900 MHz in every region by 2028, replacing 3G.

**Competitive advantages:** owns the last mile into older buildings and the largest backbone; state backing and Universal Service money; present in every district; lowest prices (about 15% below the median).

## Competitive forces (Porter's five forces)

The strongest force in Mediland is rivalry in mobile: the market is saturated (108 SIMs per 100 people) and switching takes one day. Rivalry hits Mira Mobile and Nova Wave hardest; TransMed is the most protected thanks to long B2B contracts.

| Force | Market | Sunline | Mira Mobile | MedTel | Nova Wave | TransMed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rivalry among operators | High | Medium | High | Medium | High | Low |
| Threat of new entrants | Low | Low | Medium | Medium | Medium | Low |
| Threat of substitutes | Medium | Medium | Medium | High | Low | Medium |
| Power of buyers | Medium | Medium | High | Low | High | High |
| Power of suppliers | Medium | Medium | Medium | Medium | High | Low |

- **Rivalry, high.** Two near-equal mobile leaders, a fast-growing newcomer and number portability since 2022. Competition is on bundles, 5G and service more than on price. Mira Mobile fights on two fronts: Sunline on quality and Nova Wave on data and home internet. TransMed competes in tenders, where contracts last 3-5 years.
- **New entrants, low.** Licences, spectrum and sites cost hundreds of millions of dollars. The real risk is low-cost brands without their own network (MVNOs): the regulator plans rules for them in 2027, and banks and e-wallet brands are interested. They would target the mass B2C market of Mira Mobile, MedTel and Nova Wave; MedTel and Nova Wave could also earn as their hosts.
- **Substitutes, medium.** Internet calls and messengers replace paid voice and SMS; mobile phones replace fixed lines (hardest on MedTel); mobile home routers replace DSL; satellite internet competes in remote areas and at remote business sites. Nova Wave gains from these shifts because it sells data.
- **Buyers, medium.** One household has little power alone, but multi-SIM use (1.41 per user) and one-day porting make it easy to move spend. Young urban users (Nova Wave) and price-sensitive families (Mira Mobile) switch most; rural and social-tariff users (MedTel) have few options. Large companies and government buy through tenders, so TransMed and Sunline's B2B arms face strong buyers.
- **Suppliers, medium.** A few radio vendors (Ericsson, Nokia, Huawei, ZTE, Samsung) and a switch costs years; spectrum comes from the state; fiber between cities belongs mostly to TransMed and MedTel. Nova Wave depends most: on MedTel roaming and on sharing with Mira Mobile. TransMed owns its backbone, so supplier power is low for it.

## Regulation and patterns of customer behavior

Moving to another operator is easy since number portability started in 2022, and the year has strong seasonal swings, so people who really leave are hard to tell from people who only go quiet.

**Rules of the market**

- Regulator: Mediland Communications Authority (MCA). Licences are technology-neutral, for 15 years.
- 5G spectrum auction in 2023 (3.5 GHz): Sunline 100 MHz, Mira Mobile 80 MHz, Nova Wave 80 MHz. TransMed holds local licences for private networks; MedTel did not bid. A 700 MHz auction for rural coverage is planned for 2027.
- Mobile number portability since 2022: a port takes one working day; about 0.5 M numbers move a year (2% of SIMs).
- SIM registration with digital ID since 2023, at most 10 SIMs per person. Rules for MVNOs (brands without their own network) are expected in 2027.
- Universal Service Fund: operators pay 1% of revenue; the money goes to remote towers and MedTel's social tariffs.
- Operators close prepaid numbers after 120-180 days without paid activity (each sets its own rule) and reuse a number after 6 months.

**Seasonal and behavioral patterns**

- Herders move to high pastures in June and come back in September; many stay out of coverage for months (R09-R11).
- The coast nearly doubles in summer; tourists and seasonal workers buy short-term prepaid SIMs.
- Seasonal and Belmora workers leave in October-November; some keep their SIMs, many drop them.
- Students move to big cities in September, often switching to a cheaper or city-strong operator.
- Mine rotation, 2 weeks on and 2 weeks off (Dorhan, Qasra), creates regular gaps in activity.
- Spring Day (March 21-24) and New Year bring travel and peaks in top-ups and calls.
- Multi-SIM is common (1.41 SIMs per user): a second SIM can go quiet without the person leaving the market.
- Monthly mobile churn: about 3.2% prepaid, 1.1% postpaid, 2.6% blended (about 31% a year).
